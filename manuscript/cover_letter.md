### Journal Fit and Core Contribution to Remote Sensing, Society, and Environment
Tropical peatland wildfires across equatorial Southeast Asia present one of the most pressing interdisciplinary environmental crises in the world, directly bridging physical remote sensing technologies, ecosystem degradation, and societal land governance. While statutory environmental enforcement in Indonesia emphasizes corporate strict liability (Law No. 32/2009, Article 88), recurrent mega-fires continue to release catastrophic carbon emissions and regional particulate matter (PM2.5) smoke-haze during drought pulses. 

*Remote Sensing Applications: Society and Environment* provides the ideal home for this research because our study operationalizes satellite Earth observation data to directly interrogate the societal, tenurial, and ecological dynamics governing tropical landscapes:
1. **Satellite Remote Sensing Integration:** We integrate 43,937 verified satellite thermal anomalies from the NASA FIRMS constellation (VIIRS I-Band 375-m and MODIS 1-km active fire products), Fire Radiative Power (FRP), 13 primary canal vectors digitized in metric UTM Zone 49S projection, and ECMWF ERA5-Land atmospheric reanalysis.
2. **Quasi-Experimental Spatial Boundary Discontinuity Design (Spatial BDD):** Rather than treating satellite fire observations as unstructured point clouds, we implement a Spatial BDD across $\pm 3,000$ meters of 812 audited cadastral concessions (627 oil palm HGU and 185 commercial forestry PBPH). We reveal that active fire density jumps sharply by **+56.5%** directly across certified borders onto unallocated state land (*Area Penggunaan Lain*, APL), exposing a severe institutional governance void driven by private boundary surveillance combined with unmanaged trans-boundary peat drainage desiccation.
3. **Tobler's First Law and Spatial Machine Learning:** We demonstrate that tree-based remote sensing models ingesting geodetic coordinates suffer from spatial memorization and data leakage. Under 5-fold Spatial Block Cross-Validation (`GroupKFold` by administrative regency), coordinate-dependent models collapse completely ($F_1 \to 0.00$), whereas a Pure Biophysical Model excluding spatial coordinates preserves operational fire detection across unseen regencies (Holdout ROC-AUC: 0.7527; Extreme Fire Recall: 65.82%; Spatial CV mean $F_1 = 0.1399$, a 2.40-fold retention over coordinate models).
4. **Explainable AI and Environmental Tipping Points:** Exact Shapley value decomposition using TreeSHAP resolves critical non-linear tipping points: severe fire combustion escalates rapidly within 3,000 meters of artificial drainage canals and after 14 consecutive drought days.
5. **Actionable Societal Policy Reforms:** We translate remote sensing findings into concrete environmental governance: mandating statutory 3-km perimeter buffer stewardship for concessionaires operating contiguous peat canals, regularizing APL peatlands into community-managed Peatland Management Units (KPG) / Social Forestry, and prioritizing canal blocking along primary drainage corridors.
